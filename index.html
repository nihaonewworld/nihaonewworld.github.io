01 / About

About Me

I study gene regulation through sequencing data.

My work connects transcriptomics and epigenomics with single-cell and spatial analysis. I work from experimental design and data processing through statistical analysis to biological interpretation, with an emphasis on integrating evidence across assays.

My co-first-author publications explore epigenetic and transcriptional changes during cerebral cortex development, and the relationship between histone modifications and DNA methylation in cancer. Explore the publications →

I use R, Python and Shell to turn analysis steps into reusable workflows, and collect methods, scripts and practical notes in bio_know_base. I am also learning about AI-driven drug discovery and virtual cell models.

05 / Approach

A question-first workflow

I start with the biological comparison and keep analysis decisions traceable through to the final interpretation.

  1. 01

    Frame the question

    Define the biological comparison, experimental unit, replicates and relevant covariates before choosing an analysis.

  2. 02

    Understand the data

    Review sample metadata, sequencing quality and reference compatibility. Make quality decisions explicit.

  3. 03

    Connect the evidence

    Bring statistical results, annotations and complementary omics layers together to interpret the biological question.

  4. 04

    Make it reusable

    Keep scripts, parameters, software versions and interpretation notes alongside the results.
